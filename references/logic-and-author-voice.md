# Logic and Clinical Investigator Voice

## Two logic scales

The global chain answers: Why this problem, why this design, what was observed, what can be inferred, what remains uncertain, and why the study matters?

The local chain answers: What is this paragraph's job, which fact starts it, which reasoning is valid, and what conclusion follows?

Test each paragraph without connectors. If the relation disappears after removing “however”, “therefore”, “moreover”, or “notably”, repair the reasoning or order before restoring any necessary signpost.

Common broken chains include:

- background fact → unrelated experiment → causal conclusion;
- co-expression → pathway enrichment → mechanism proved;
- statistical significance → clinical usefulness;
- public dataset direction → independent validation;
- limitation → generic future research with no link to the uncertainty;
- several citations → broad claim none of them directly supports.

## Default voice

Clinical Investigator Voice should sound like a clinician-researcher who understands the disease, generated or reviewed the data, knows the study's limits, and speaks to other medical researchers.

Prefer:

- the study subject before the workflow;
- concrete findings before methodological self-commentary;
- familiar clinical and scientific words;
- stable names for groups, outcomes, genes, assays, and models;
- one main idea per sentence;
- short sentences when one sentence is enough;
- proportionate limitations placed where they affect interpretation.

Avoid writing as a data auditor, pipeline operator, legal reviewer, grant slogan generator, or omniscient narrator.

## Structured whole, flexible parts

Keep the manuscript's main argument stable. Within that structure, allow natural variation: some paragraphs may be short, an important negative result may receive extra attention, and a clinically meaningful observation may appear as a genuine insight. Do not force identical paragraph shapes or a repeated finding-limit-future template.

Human voice is not simulated carelessness. Never add mistakes, vague statistics, unsupported intuitions, false memories, or deliberate inconsistency.
